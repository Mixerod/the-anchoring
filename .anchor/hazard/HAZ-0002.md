# HAZ-0002: One field named two concepts, and the validator found it before a reader did

## What happened

The first adopter carried `owner:` on all 70 of its work items, with values like `claude`,
`agent`, and `antigravity`.

Every one of them failed the `owner` shape check, which requires `@handle` or `team:<name>`.
Seventy failures from one field is not seventy mistakes; it is one modelling error stated
seventy times.

## Root cause

`owner` was specified to mean *the person or team accountable for this entity*, in the
CODEOWNERS sense. What the adopter had recorded under that name was *which agent executed
the work* — a different fact about a different kind of actor, sharing a spelling.

The schema had no way to express the second concept, so it went into the field whose name
was closest. Nothing objected until a validator with a shape ran over it.

## Prevention

A second field, not a looser first one. `src/model.ts` adds `executed_by` to `WORK`, and
`owner` keeps its shape.

Widening `owner` to accept `claude` was the available shortcut and was rejected: an
ownership field that accepts anything identifies nobody, and the failure was a missing
concept rather than a strict rule. Constraining `executed_by` to a handle shape would repeat
the same error facing the other way, so it is a free string on purpose. The reasoning is
recorded at the field in `SCALAR_FIELDS` rather than only here, because that is where the
next person will be tempted to tighten it.

Fixed in `8c3aa3f`.

## Why this is filed as `schema-gap`

Under the closed evidence classes in `src/model.ts`, this is `schema-gap`: the schema could
not express what had to be expressed. It is recorded here as a local incident rather than an
escalation, because this repository is where the schema lives — there is no upstream to
attribute it to.

The general lesson is not about `owner`. It is that a validator with a shape converts a
modelling error into a loud, countable failure, whereas a field that accepts anything would
have absorbed both meanings silently and left two concepts fused under one name for as long
as the project lived.
